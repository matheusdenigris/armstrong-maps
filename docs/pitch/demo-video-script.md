# Demo video script

A draft by Claude for Matheus. The judges are international, so everything is in English.

**Check before recording:** the format and length in the Space Apps 2026 submission guide. Earlier
editions asked for a video of about 30 seconds or up to 7 slides. There are two versions below,
30 seconds and 2 minutes; if the limit is 30 seconds, use the first only.

Record on https://www.armstrongmaps.us at Gale crater: it has a rover traverse, weather and its own
visor photograph.

## Rules for what is said

- Show only what is on `main` on the day of recording. Space weather, the Photos tab, the docs page
  and the colour keys go in only if their pull requests have been merged.
- Say "simulated" when the astronaut or the walk clock is on screen.
- Do not say the route avoids obstacles: it is a straight line between waypoints.
- Do not show the NASA logo or say the project is NASA's. Say "NASA data" or "NASA open data".

## 30-second version

| Time | On screen | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 s | The globe turning, a finger dragging it | "An astronaut on Mars needs one map to plan a safe walk. This is Armstrong Maps." |
| 5-12 s | Choose Gale; the map opens; turn on the thermal and relief layers | "Real NASA layers, from imagery to thermal and topography, stacked over the landing site." |
| 12-20 s | Tap three waypoints; the verdict goes from go to caution | "Tap to plan the route. Every stop is checked against life support, the walk-back limit and the radiation dose." |
| 20-27 s | Start the walk; open the visor | "The astronaut follows the same plan in a helmet view, in step with the ship." |
| 27-30 s | The Armstrong Maps logo | "Armstrong Maps. Built on NASA open data." |

## 2-minute version

**1. The problem (0:00-0:15)**
On screen: the globe turning, the blue coverage outlines.
Voice: "A walk on Mars is limited by the suit: hours of life support, how far you can walk back,
how much radiation you take. Planning it means crossing many datasets. Armstrong Maps puts them in
one place, on a phone."

**2. Choosing the site (0:15-0:30)**
On screen: drag the globe, choose Gale, the map opens.
Voice: "The blue outlines are where NASA publishes high-resolution mosaics. We pick Gale crater,
where Curiosity has been driving since 2012."

**3. Layers (0:30-0:55)**
On screen: Layers tab; zoom down to the HiRISE rocks; turn on the relief and the daytime infrared;
change the opacity.
Voice: "Every layer is real data from NASA's Mars Trek: Viking, CTX and HiRISE imagery down to 25
centimetres per pixel, MOLA and HRSC topography, THEMIS and TES thermal and spectral maps. The
white line is Curiosity's real traverse."
If the colour keys are merged: show one and say "Each colour has its meaning, its unit and its
source."

**4. Planning the walk (0:55-1:20)**
On screen: Walk tab; tap three waypoints; show distance, time and dose; add a waypoint past 5 km
and watch the verdict turn to no-go.
Voice: "Tap to add stops. The plan comes back go, caution or no-go, with the reasons: eight hours
of life support with a 20 percent reserve, a five-kilometre walk-back limit, and the dose measured
by Curiosity's RAD instrument. The suit limits are our assumptions, and the app says so."
If space weather is merged: open the Site tab and say "Space weather comes from NASA's DONKI. When
a solar storm is forecast to reach Mars, the plan turns to caution."

**5. The astronaut's visor (1:20-1:45)**
On screen: Start the walk; open the visor; the heading tape turns and the distance drops.
Voice: "The astronaut sees the same plan in a helmet view: heading, next waypoint, life support,
dose and a round map turned to where they face. The photograph is a real rover panorama; the walk
is simulated."

**6. Close (1:45-2:00)**
On screen: back to the console with the whole route; the logo.
Voice: "Safety first, built on NASA open data, on any phone. Armstrong Maps."

## Recording checklist

- A clean browser with no personal tabs.
- A phone held upright for the visor and the map, or a desktop for everything.
- Record each part on its own and edit later; keep 10 percent of the time spare.
